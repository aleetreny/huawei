# Career event decisions

Choose the best available option for this situation rather than recognizing a previously seen event.

## Compare the options

1. Read the event and all currently offered choices. Separate explicit information from assumptions about outcomes.
2. Apply the current risk assessment. Compare likely effects on health, dignity, capability, relationships, delivery, finances and unresolved obligations. Consider both immediate changes and future consequences.
3. Prefer an option that satisfies the immediate need while preserving sustainable growth and future options. If one option plausibly improves several dimensions with fewer liabilities, prefer it over a narrower gain unless the current context supplies a reason not to.
4. When choices conflict, address a credible urgent threat first. Otherwise seek capability and credible delivery supported by healthy relationships and manageable commitments. A gain in visible output is not sufficient justification for avoidable harm.

## Reason about workplace trade-offs

- Prefer credible commitments and honest communication over concealing problems, exaggerating progress or accepting work without a feasible plan.
- Consider respectful boundaries, direct communication, appropriate escalation and repair of relationships where these options address the actual problem. Do not assume any one communication style wins every situation.
- Balance learning against delivery and workload. Neither always taking the difficult technical route nor always choosing immediate convenience is a universal strategy.
- Consider whether an apparent financial or career benefit creates dependence, misconduct, stress or delayed obligations.
- For a follow-up, account for commitments already made in this run. New feedback can justify changing course; do not continue a damaging choice solely to appear consistent.
- If all offered options have drawbacks, choose the most manageable downside in the current state. Do not invent an unlisted action.

## Act and learn

Submit the exact currently offered choice identifier through `take_action`, with a concise note stating the main trade-off and any uncertainty. Do not put secrets or claims about hidden formulas in notes.

Observe the result before another decision. If the visible outcome or warning differs from expectations, update the current-run assessment. Use logs when needed to distinguish an immediate effect from a delayed consequence. Do not convert one observed outcome into a fixed answer for future events.

After an uncertain tool response, observe before retrying; a repeated action may advance the game twice. When the session has ended, stop taking actions and report the observed outcome.
