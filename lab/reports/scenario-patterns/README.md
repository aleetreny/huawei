# Scenario families and first-promotion patterns

26 October 2026. Offline analysis of the complete encountered catalogue and its seven public-tool evidence archives. No new games or model quota were used. This explains what is useful for first-review promotion without confusing an observed post-review state with a causal rule.

## Six recurring decision mechanisms

The taxonomy is a human interpretation of the dominant decision mechanism, not an automatically discovered causal clustering. Each of the 89 titles is assigned to one primary family for reproducible counts; the mechanisms overlap. The exhaustive assignment is in [taxonomy.json](taxonomy.json).

| Primary family | Titles | Titles observed before the review | Stages | Answers | Transferable decision pattern |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Delivery and technical execution | 22 | 16 | 52 | 119 | Produce a verifiable result, resolve the actual defect, negotiate feasible scope and complete follow-through. |
| Collaboration and boundaries | 20 | 20 | 49 | 121 | Cooperate without accepting unlimited work or destroying working relationships. |
| Trust, disclosure and integrity | 18 | 12 | 51 | 120 | Protect confidentiality, correct false claims and avoid creating an unresolved liability for a temporary gain. |
| Recognition, credit and career negotiation | 15 | 10 | 40 | 98 | Make genuine contribution visible, give accurate credit and seek clear terms. |
| Health and sustainable capacity | 8 | 4 | 14 | 34 | Preserve enough capacity for the next events rather than recovering only at collapse. |
| Learning, knowledge sharing and mentorship | 6 | 2 | 7 | 18 | Build capability sustainably; additional study does not repair a delivery or relationship gap by itself. |

**57 of 89 titles have multiple observed stages.** The useful unit of reasoning is therefore a decision with a possible obligation, cost or later branch, not a standalone question. The next stage can turn an apparent gain into a problem if delivery, disclosure, recovery or follow-up was omitted.

## Time is the first causal filter

All **1,940 observed first-review transitions** occur at the clock change **5 → 6**; all have grade **C**. Promotion is a separate outcome: **682 episodes promoted**, despite that grade. Fifty-one of those episodes died before the end of the six-month window; 631 were promoted and still alive.

In this dataset, **25 of 89 titles occur only in month 6, after the first review**, including all four families of the five recently closed answers. Their occurrence or answer cannot explain an earlier first-review promotion in these histories. In particular, advanced graduate mentoring, retention packages, redundancy-list disclosure and palpitations follow-ups should not be mistaken for first-promotion prerequisites just because they are interesting or rare. The complete late-only list and month counts are in [statistics.json](statistics.json). These are observed eligibility patterns, not an inspected engine rule.

Before the first review there is only one observed quarterly allocation/main-action opportunity, in month 3. The month-6 opportunity is too late for that decision. The practical objective is to arrive ready before the transition and preserve readiness through the remaining story choices.

No specific early narrative title occurs in every promoted trajectory. This argues against a required event-specific ritual; it does not establish that latent relationship support is irrelevant.

## Growth readiness is a combination, not a single maximised statistic

The earlier exploratory proxy was Skill ≥ 8, Network ≥ 3, Output ≥ 3, with dignity kept away from exhaustion. These are private development observations, not published cutoffs or submission instructions.

There are **1,595 review transitions without a simultaneous explicit “Old landmines” or “Trust crisis” message**. Their post-transition states give this descriptive table:

| Post-transition profile | Promoted / observations |
|---|---:|
| Skill ≥ 8, Network ≥ 3, Output ≥ 3, Dignity ≥ 2 | **587 / 587** |
| Same growth profile, Dignity 0–1 | **1 / 16** |
| A gap in at least one growth dimension, any dignity | **0 / 992** |

This is a strong observed signature, **not an identified promotion formula or a guarantee**. The numbers are measured after an action response that may bundle review, clipping, salary, warnings and other consequences. The filtered subset also excludes difficult transitions. Do not paste these hidden numerical cutoffs into a Skill.

**The previous exact separator has now been falsified.** Closure batch 1, run 295, promoted with post-transition Skill/Network/Output 8/3/3 and Dignity 1, without either of the explicit explosion messages above. Before the action dignity was 3; the chosen action's public log reported Dignity +1, while the observed post-response dignity was 1. Other settlement effects are therefore present. The old audit remains a correct frozen-sample record, but its numerical separator is no longer exact on the enlarged dataset. This is a concrete reason to track trends and feedback rather than enforce a supposed Dignity=2 promotion gate.

## A useful signal available before the decision

In the frozen 512-episode learned-policy batch, 503 episodes reached an observable start of month 5. Using the growth proxy above at that moment, before the review:

| Start-of-month-5 state | Later promoted |
|---|---:|
| All three growth dimensions already ready | **237 / 307 (77.2%)** |
| At least one growth gap | **99 / 196 (50.5%)** |

These are observational associations within one research bot, not randomised interventions on readiness and not the effect of the prose Skill. They are nevertheless more useful for a live agent than the post-review table: early readiness is associated with a higher promotion rate, but 70 initially ready careers still failed to promote and 99 initially unready careers recovered. Readiness must be maintained, and a gap is a reason to redirect effort rather than resign.

The research bot uses an empirical lookup; its results cannot be attributed to a general reasoning agent. The frozen prose-only Haiku comparison remains unplayed by Matteo's manual-resumption choice.

## Investments: check actual benefit and opportunity cost

Across the seven archives, positive quarterly investments at L1 produced zero net visible gain **2,039 times at Network 3**, **1,797 times at Output 3**, and **812 times at Skill 9**. These are repeated actions in correlated histories, not independent experiments or an unconditional game-wide ceiling. Earlier observations can exceed some of these values, and automatic settlement can offset an action's nominal effect.

The operational lesson is to check the realised change after every investment. If the target dimension repeatedly does not improve, inspect feedback and other benefits, then direct the next scarce action towards a remaining gap, capacity or unresolved exposure. Repeating key projects or skill learning automatically is not a promotion strategy.

The public action menu also makes useful tradeoffs explicit. Preparing report materials costs one Energy and advertises Output +1/Dignity +1. Two such actions advertise the same Output increase as high-intensity work's two-Energy action, while avoiding its advertised Health/Dignity losses and HiddenRisk increase. This is a comparison of published direct effects, subject to actual settlement, not proof of total strategic dominance. Preserve a needed recovery or risk-cleanup action rather than filling every Energy point with reports.

The controlled main-action arms in the learned-policy batch reinforce the absence of a unique required button: mentorship promoted 89/128, key project 88/128, and no main action 78/128. Differences are exploratory, with unpaired game histories. They do not establish a mandatory mentor, mandatory project, or a hidden sponsorship formula. A choice should address the current gap.

Across all 1,152 recorded eliminations, the official failure labels were 576 trust crises, 546 blackouts, 23 broke careers, six relationship-related resignations and one obsolescence. Trust and physical collapse account for 97.4% of this sample's deaths. This is a mix of different research controllers, including deliberately harmful exploration; it is not a natural failure distribution for a reasoning agent. It nevertheless explains why the promotion objective needs explicit risk and capacity safeguards.

## General operational priorities

1. Track capability, collaboration and credited delivery together. Name the actual gap before committing scarce resources.
2. Complete preparation before the observed review boundary. Do not plan on the next quarter's actions rescuing the current decision.
3. Preserve dignity and health while growing. Capacity and self-respect are safeguards, not dimensions to cash out without a plan.
4. Treat scope, promises, defects, disclosures and relationship ruptures as possible delayed obligations. Record the next owner/action and close the loop.
5. Make real results visible with evidence and accurate credit. Do not manufacture visibility through false claims or unsustainable availability.
6. Observe actual gains and new warnings after acting. Redirect effort when an investment stalls; distinguish a performance grade, a promotion notice and continued survival.

These priorities are a supported working hypothesis for a transferable agent rule. They have not demonstrated a universal promotion guarantee or a winning Skill on the evaluated model.
