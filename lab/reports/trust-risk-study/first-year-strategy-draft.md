# First-year strategy — general research draft

This is a proposed synthesis of public-tool research, not a validated Skill or a guarantee. The controlled study evaluated a frozen empirical bot with a response-history lookup, not an agent reading these paragraphs. No event mappings, hidden numerical cutoffs or prescribed calendar triggers are part of the guidance below.

## Balance progress and the ability to continue

Build capability, usable relationships and credited delivery together. Identify the current bottleneck from actual state changes and feedback. Choose feasible work with clear ownership and a verifiable result, while preserving health, dignity and the money needed to meet foreseeable obligations. Recognition bought through unaffordable personal spending can undermine the career it is intended to support.

Before choosing, name the intended benefit, its cost and the downside you can absorb. After choosing, inspect the realised change and any new warning. If an investment repeatedly stops helping, redirect effort to another gap, capacity or an unresolved obligation. Reassess this diagnosis after a promotion: both the growth bar and the usefulness of previous investments may change.

## Keep incident history separate from current warnings

Maintain separate records for current warnings, unresolved commitments and previous serious incidents. A risk-reduction action or a calmer warning does not establish that the consequences of an earlier incident have disappeared. Keep the incident in memory unless public evidence establishes that it no longer matters.

After a serious incident, raise the standard for accepting a shortcut, disclosure or promise. Prefer an option whose safeguards, validation, ownership and follow-through you can explain. Do not treat a small visible gain as sufficient compensation for exposure you cannot absorb. A temporary improvement in the panel does not prove that trust has been restored.

Use available risk reduction when the situation warrants it, and address the source of the exposure as well. Cleanup is not permission to resume the same behaviour that created the problem. Repeating cleanup mechanically can also displace useful growth and recovery.

## Protect commitments and resources across events

Keep a compact ledger: what remains open, who owns the next action, what resources it requires and what public evidence would close it. Return to the ledger when a related issue reappears. Protect confidential information, correct misleading claims and report material delivery risks with a feasible plan.

Track the affordability of recurring favours, personal contributions and uncertain reimbursements from the start of the career. Do not wait until after promotion to protect the reserve. When money is tight, seek sustainable arrangements and clear terms rather than accepting discretionary costs for immediate approval. This is a research-motivated safeguard; its incremental effect has not yet been isolated in the controlled study.

## Prepare before a review and consolidate afterwards

Use the handbook and observed clock transitions to prepare before the decision boundary. Do not depend on an allocation that arrives after the review. At the review, inspect grade, level change, warnings and survival separately.

After advancement, check whether working relationships or responsibilities have changed. Rebuild alignment and support where necessary while maintaining delivery and capacity. A promotion is a new state to manage, not evidence that every earlier liability has been resolved.
