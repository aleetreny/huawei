# New hire handbook

**Document type**: initialization
**Role**: Handbook issued when you join the product R&D organisation
**Audience**: New hires

Welcome to the product engineering group. Before your career clock starts, this handbook sets out the public rules. Everything in it is part of the published game design, and you can use it for long-term planning.

## What is published

- **Published**: This handbook covers the published rules: the level ladder, visible stats, quarterly energy and main actions, the timeline and promotion windows, performance grades, risk and failure rules, and monthly pay.
- **Not published**: Not listed here. You can only infer these from emails, meeting notes, manager feedback and outcomes: promotion score thresholds, talk tiers, performance formula weights, hidden risk and random event odds.
- **Note**: You cannot see everything that is happening in the organisation. Plan across quarters and years with incomplete information.

## Levels

There are ten levels, L1 to L10. Higher levels expect broader impact and set a higher bar for promotion.

A promotion window opens every 6 months. The bar depends on Skill, Network, Output and level; the exact thresholds are hidden.

| Level | Name | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Junior engineer | Carries out tasks with clear guidance |
| L2 | Engineer | Owns modules independently |
| L3 | Senior engineer | Owns core modules, with growing technical responsibility |
| L4 | Staff engineer | Works on critical systems with major project impact |
| L5 | Principal / team lead | Leads a subsystem or small team |
| L6 | Senior principal / tech lead | Leads large programmes or sets technical direction |
| L7 | Director of engineering | Department-level technical strategy |
| L8 | Senior director | Cross-department technical planning |
| L9 | Distinguished fellow / architecture committee | Company-wide technical influence |
| L10 | Chief architect / fellow | Makes company-wide technical decisions over a long horizon |

Shown on your panel or in the monthly summary. These are your main inputs for decisions.

| Key | Name | Range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health | Health | 0–10 | Physical and mental stamina. Long-term overwork wears it down |
| Dignity | Dignity | 0–10 | Self-respect and recognition at work. Staying low for too long triggers a crisis |
| Skill | Skill | 0–unbounded | Professional capability |
| Network | Network | 0–unbounded | The working relationships you can draw on |
| Level | Level | L1–L10 | Current career level |
| Output | Output | 0–unbounded | The visible results you have built up over time |
| Wealth | Wealth | unbounded | Cumulative wealth |
| Energy | Energy | 0–3 | Your energy budget for the quarter, spent on quarterly actions |
| CurrentLevelDuration | Months at current level | 0–unbounded | Consecutive months at present level |

## Time

- Length: 48 months
- Story events per month: 3
- Each month:
  - 1. A new month starts and you are paid the base salary for your level
  - 2. Up to 3 story events are drawn from the pool
  - 3. You play through the story events and make your choices
  - 4. At the end of a quarter (months 3, 6, 9…), once the month’s event quota is full, you move on to quarterly energy actions
  - 5. Allocate your 3 Energy points for the quarter. You can pick the same action more than once, until you finish or skip
  - 6. Choose one quarterly main action (or none)
  - 7. Your stats are settled. Crossing a red line may bring a written or verbal warning
  - 8. Every half year: a 1:1 with your manager (no decision needed)
  - 9. Every 6 months: formal performance review and bonus

## Energy actions

3 Energy per quarter. Unused Energy does not roll over.

- Energy each quarter: 3
- Unused Energy carries over: no

| Action | Energy cost | Stat change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High-intensity work | 2 | Output +, Health -, Dignity - |
| Skill learning | 2 | Skill +, Health - |
| Network maintenance | 1 | Network +, Dignity + |
| Defuse legacy risk | 2 | None (or an effect you cannot see) |
| Mental recovery | 1 | Dignity + |
| Health recovery | 1 | Health + |
| Prepare report materials | 1 | Output +, Dignity + |

## Main actions

Each quarter, pick one main action or none.

- Most you can pick each quarter: 1
- You may take no main action: yes

| Action | Stat change |
| --- | --- |
| Apply for a key project | Output +, Skill +, Network + |
| Report results to your manager | Output +, Network +, Dignity + |
| Fight for resources | Output +, Network + |
| Apply for rotation | Skill +, Network + |
| Showcase results | Output +, Dignity + |
| Build a mentorship | Skill +, Network + |
| Repair a key relationship | Network +, Dignity + |
| Lay low and reduce risk | Health + |

## Performance review

- How often: Formal review every 6 months
- Note: Performance combines Skill, Network, Output, level, etc.; bonus is a multiple of base monthly pay. The bands are published in advance and applied the same way to everyone.

| Grade | Meaning | Bonus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S | Exceptional | base monthly ×3 |
| A | Strong | base monthly ×2 |
| B | Solid | base monthly ×1 |
| C | Weak | base monthly ×0.25 |
| D | Very weak | no bonus |

## Risk and failure

The red lines are not in the onboarding slides. Old hands will tell you what tends to blow up. The timing and counts you learn by playing.

### What people say

- Old landmines all go off at once, and half a year of work vanishes!
- In a review your teammates use jargon you cannot keep up with. You nod and promise to look into it…

### Ways people say a run ends

- Blackout — your body gives out before the KPIs do. Game over
- Breakdown — after months with no dignity left, you send a resignation email
- Trust crisis — after a second blow-up, the company says you have used up its trust
- Dismissed for capability — two weak half-year reviews and you hand the badge in
- Isolated — work that needs other people cannot get done, and you resign
- Broke — months with an empty wallet
- Obsolete — your manager suggests a role that matches what you can actually do

Survive all 48 months and you may earn a career summary worth showing.
