## Turn loop
1. `observe`; read `events` first (HR remark, review talk, warnings).
2. Find the rule that applies below, then `take_action` with the exact `choice` number and a short note.
3. If `success` is false, pick another option. Stop when the game ends.

## How to choose a story option
1. Read the context: months to the next review month, the latest HR remark, Wealth, months left.
2. Strike out every option a rule below forbids in this context.
3. Among the options left, pick the one that best builds Skill, Network and Output without costing Health or Dignity you cannot spare. If every option is struck out, pick the one that breaks the fewest rules.
4. In the take_action notes write which options you struck out and why, in a few words.

## Rules
Rules are listed in priority order: when two apply, the earlier one wins.

1. **Close the career well.** The career ends after month 48 (the published length); the ending evaluation counts level, wealth, health and dignity, skill, risk control and network. In the quarterly phase of that final month: main action Lay low and reduce risk; Energy first on Defuse legacy risk if the HR remark is not clear, then Health recovery and Mental recovery until Health and Dignity are full, then Network maintenance. Never High-intensity work or Skill learning there: their gains come too late and their Health cost counts. In the stories of the last months, pick the option that keeps you healthy, respected and out of trouble.
2. **Answer the HR remark.** Each month an HR partner comments on your risk. Only the smiling, all-right remark means clear. Any remark that mentions risk, worry or anger means hidden risk is piling up; it blows up when it gets high, and a second blow-up ends the run. While the remark is not the clear one, in every quarterly phase: the first Energy action is Defuse legacy risk (unless Health is nearly gone) and the main action is Lay low and reduce risk. This comes before growth, before Skill and before any review plan.
3. **Protect the review.** Reviews come every six months and are settled as the review month begins, before that month's stories, so the story choices of the month just before a review month are the last ones that count. In that month never pick an option that may lower Skill, Network or Output, even if another option looks more principled or adds Dignity. Before choosing, mark each option: does it go against a person (blaming, correcting, refusing, reporting someone)? Then it costs Network. Does it take time from your own delivery (extra or unplanned work, investigations, rework, the slow thorough path, stopping to fix someone else's problem)? Then it costs Output. Pick an option with neither mark; if every option has one, pick the one whose loss is on a stat that is not holding your promotion back.
4. **No detonators under risk.** While the HR remark is not the clear one, strike out every option that hides or denies something, lies or invents, gossips or leaks, cuts a corner, or starts a formal process (formal complaints, investigations, open letters, going over your manager's head). The more worried the remark, the more you prefer the quietest option left that is not dishonest. Use your own judgement among the options that remain.
5. **Do not go broke early.** Pay is small in the first levels and Wealth starts near zero. While Wealth is only a few months of pay, refuse options that cost money (paying, buying, treating, lending, fees, gifts). Months with Wealth below zero end the run.
6. **Build Skill at senior levels.** From L4 on, Skill is the slow stat that holds promotion back; Network and Output keep up on their own. In every quarterly phase at L4 or above (except the career's final month), spend 2 Energy on Skill learning and take Apply for rotation as the main action. In stories, prefer the option that teaches or builds capability.

## Reading what an option costs
Option effects are hidden; read them from the wording. Invented examples (not from the game):
- "Correct the director in front of the client" lowers Network. "Raise it with the director privately afterwards" keeps it.
- "Spend the week rebuilding a colleague's slides" lowers Output: your own delivery slips. "Point them to the template and finish your release" keeps it.
- "Agree to the impossible date to keep the peace" or "say nothing and hope" add hidden risk. "State calmly what can be done by when" is safe.
- "Quietly fix it and tell nobody" hides a problem: hidden risk. "Fix it and note it in the ticket" is safe.
