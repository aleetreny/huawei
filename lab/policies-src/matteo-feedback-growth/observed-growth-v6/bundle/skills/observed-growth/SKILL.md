## Why growth comes first
The handbook ties promotion to Skill, Network, Output and level, and pay and review bonuses rise with level. Each level has a ceiling: a stat that stops rising despite investment is at its ceiling for this level, and it resumes growing after the next promotion. Promotion needs Skill, Network and Output all at their ceilings when the review is applied; one point below on any of them usually means no promotion, and staying at the same level brings weaker reviews. Two things end careers anyway: hidden risk that blows up, and exhausted Health.

## Review timing
A formal review and promotion window are applied at the very start of the review month, before that month's story events and before its quarter-end allocation. So the allocation before a review is the previous quarter-end, and every story choice between that allocation and the review counts. Read the handbook once at the start to know the review cycle, and track at each quarter-end whether the next review comes before the next quarter-end.

## Turn loop
Each turn: observe, read feedback and the event, choose an offered affordable option, act with a short note, observe again. If an action is rejected, observe before retrying. Stop at the explicit career ending.

## Story events
Most stat gains come from story choices. Prefer the option that takes ownership, delivers a concrete result, builds capability, helps a colleague visibly or makes your contribution known. Never choose options that cut corners, overpromise, hide a problem, take undue credit, bend rules, or confront and blame colleagues: they feed hidden risk and cost relationships. Decline only when taking part would cause a serious Health drop or an integrity, safety or confidentiality breach. Disclose real defects and fix them completely.
**Before a review:** when the next review comes before the next quarter-end, guard Network and Output. Avoid options that strain a relationship, drop or delay a deliverable, or withdraw from shared work, unless the alternative breaches integrity or safety.

## Quarter-end allocation
Write a short note: Skill, Network, Output and Health now, their best values at this level, and at the previous quarter-end; risk signals since then; whether the next review comes before the next quarter-end. Then allocate in this order.

1. **Risk cleanup.** If HR, your manager, a doctor or an event mentioned risk, warnings, piled-up issues or an incident since the previous quarter-end, spend Defuse legacy risk first, even when the remark says it is not serious yet. Its effect is invisible but real.
2. **Health reserve.** Spend Health recovery when Health is at or below the middle of its range, or when the planned spending would put it there. Do not recover a near-full reserve.
3. **Growth** with the remaining Energy and the main action:
   - If the next review comes before the next quarter-end: top up every input that is below its best value at this level. Network maintenance for Network; Prepare report materials for Output; main action Report results to your manager or Apply for a key project. Skill learning only if Skill is below its best.
   - Otherwise build: Skill learning, Network maintenance; main action Apply for rotation, Build a mentorship or Apply for a key project.
   - If Skill did not rise since the previous quarter-end, it is at its ceiling: skip Skill learning until the next promotion and put that Energy into Network or Output.
   - If Skill, Network and Output all stayed flat, swap the repeated main action for a different growth action.

## After a weak review
A second weak review ends the career, so the next review decides survival, and promotion is what breaks a run of weak reviews. Until it, every quarter targets the inputs below their ceiling plus visible Output. Keep risk cleanup and the Health reserve; do not Lay low.

## Wealth
Promotion is the main source of wealth. In offered pay or role talks, cite actual results and market evidence and ask for concrete terms. Accept bounded extra responsibility that comes with recognition.
