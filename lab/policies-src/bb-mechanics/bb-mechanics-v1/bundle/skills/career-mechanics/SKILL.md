# Climb steadily, never blow up

## How the game works
- **Story answers decide most of your career.** They carry most of the growth, and careless answers are what end runs. Quarterly actions only adjust.
- **Promotion is checked at the review moment.** A half-year review looks at Skill, Network and Output as they stand when the review month begins, before that month's events and its quarter-end allocation. All three must be high enough for your level; one short input blocks the promotion. Low Dignity blocks it too, and built-up hidden risk makes it less likely.
- **Every level has a ceiling for each growth input.** A gain beyond it is lost. You have hit a ceiling when an action that should raise an input does not change it. Being at all three ceilings when a review begins is the strongest position you can reach.
- **Weak reviews come from staying put.** Grades improve as you climb, and repeated weak reviews end the run, so an early promotion out of the entry level matters more than anything else early on.
- **Hidden risk explodes.** It is built by answers, not shown as a number. The HR partner's monthly remark measures it: calm is fine; any concern means it is building. An explosion costs Dignity, Wealth and recent results; a second one ends the run.

## Every turn
1. `observe`. Note Health, Dignity, Skill, Network, Output, Wealth, your level, and how soon the next review month begins.
2. Read new feedback first: the HR remark, warnings, review results.
3. Apply the rules below, then `take_action` with the choice number and a short note: weakest input, risk state, reason.

## Story answers
1. **Reject** answers that conceal or delay a problem, deny or misrepresent, gossip or leak, cut corners or bet that a known problem will not surface, or publicly blame someone. They build hidden risk fast and usually cost Dignity or Network.
2. Read what each remaining answer builds:
   - Skill: learning, doing the work properly, putting quality first, owning the fix.
   - Network: helping, collaborating, aligning people, giving credit.
   - Output: keeping your time on your own deliverables. Volunteering for side work, extra coaching and rework take Output away.
   - Dignity: being transparent, following the proper process, protecting confidential information, setting fair boundaries.
   - Health: rest. Overwork drains it.
3. Pick the safe answer that builds the input furthest below where your level needs it, without costing Health or Dignity when either is low. Passive or people-pleasing answers cost Dignity and Network: avoid them.
4. Between honest answers, prefer the tactful one: correct things in writing or after the meeting, steer back without a scene, share your reasoning with the team. A public stand or confrontation costs Network even when you are right.
5. **In the month before a review month, keeping Skill, Network and Output intact comes first.** Accept a cost in Health, Dignity or comfort to avoid losing a point of any of them: those recover with the next quarterly actions, a lost point costs the promotion. Still never pick an answer that hides, deceives or blames, and do not let Health fall near zero.

## Climbing
- Bring Skill, Network and Output to their ceilings and hold them there into each review.
- If Network and Output sit at their ceilings and you are still not promoted, Skill is what is missing: raise it every month with skill-building answers, Apply for rotation or Build a mentorship, and Skill learning while Health is comfortable.

## Quarterly actions
- Health not comfortable: Health recovery first. Risk concern or a warning: Defuse legacy risk; if the concern repeats, also Lay low and reduce risk as the main action.
- Then lift the input furthest below its ceiling: Prepare report materials for Output, Network maintenance for Network, Skill learning for Skill. Main action: whichever adds to the inputs you lack; Apply for a key project adds to all three.
- Avoid High-intensity work: its hidden risk costs more than the Output it buys.
- Spend all Energy; it does not roll over.

## Risk discipline
- Built-up risk goes off when something exposes it: a cover-up or excuse that can unravel, or a step that puts you under formal scrutiny (a signed complaint, a formal investigation, a stand-off with management). With a clean record such principled stands are safe; while the HR remark shows concern, Defuse first and avoid both.
- After an explosion, take no risky answer for the rest of the run, and Defuse at every quarter while concern remains.
- Towards the end of the run, close open risks and recover Health and Dignity: the ending counts them.
