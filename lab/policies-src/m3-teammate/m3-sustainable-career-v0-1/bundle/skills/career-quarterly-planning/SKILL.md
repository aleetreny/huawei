# Career quarterly planning

Use the actual phase, choices and remaining Energy returned by the official interface. The public rules provide a quarterly budget of three Energy points, no carry-over and at most one main action. Do not assume an unaffordable action is available or that the main-action phase has the same costs as Energy actions.

## Plan the Energy phase

Read the current risk assessment and decide which need is most pressing:

- Declining health or workload exhaustion: consider health recovery before activities that consume more health.
- Dignity problems: compare mental recovery with actions that also strengthen relationships or credible results.
- Relationship weakness: consider network maintenance, especially when collaboration is becoming difficult.
- Capability concerns: consider learning while accounting for its Energy and health cost.
- Warnings about unresolved organizational liabilities: consider defusing legacy risk even though its benefit may not appear as an immediate statistic increase. Do not select it automatically without context or assume it clears every risk.
- Sustainable state with a results gap: consider report preparation or other available growth actions, accounting for their costs and trade-offs.

These are conditional considerations, not a permanent action ranking. Compare combinations within the actual remaining budget. Favor useful improvements across multiple dimensions when they also address the active need. Avoid spending a whole phase on a single statistic simply because it is easy to increase.

After each Energy action, observe again. Update priorities if the need is resolved or new feedback appears. Repeating an action can be reasonable when the need persists; it is not a mandatory schedule. Since unused Energy does not carry over, spend it when an available action has a worthwhile benefit without an unacceptable downside. Finish the phase when no worthwhile affordable option remains.

## Choose the main action

Use the current state after Energy spending. Compare the available actions against the largest unresolved gap: sustainable delivery, capability, relationships, visibility, recovery or project opportunity. Do not assume the action with the longest list of positive effects is always best; address the present constraint and any commitments it may create.

Consider the approaching review, the level's broader expectations and the remaining career horizon without inventing promotion thresholds. Earlier investments may have time to compound; late choices should still preserve survival and the final condition of all principal dimensions.

Submit one available main action or the offered no-action option when justified. Observe its outcome and retain a short lesson for the next quarter. Do not replay a memorized quarterly sequence.
