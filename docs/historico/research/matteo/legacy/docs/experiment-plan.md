# Strategy experiments

## What is known

The public rules list six ending dimensions: career level, wealth, health and dignity, professional skill, risk control and workplace network. Their relative weights and internal scoring formulas are not published. Output also matters for performance and promotion; it is not separately listed among the six ending dimensions. The ending report exposes contributions for that local run, not guaranteed official weights.

Prioritize urgent deterioration and warning feedback before discretionary growth. This is a design hypothesis grounded in the public failure directions, not an official fixed ranking. Health, dignity, relationships, finances, skill and performance can all become survival constraints.

## Candidate Skill responsibilities

1. State assessment and risk triage: inspect visible state, recent warnings and trends; identify the most urgent problem without inventing hidden thresholds.
2. Event decisions: assess the available options in context, including reversibility, immediate effects and longer-term liabilities.
3. Quarterly planning: allocate the available energy and main action according to current weaknesses and career needs.
4. Review and adaptation: use performance reviews and observed outcomes to update priorities over the full horizon.

These are responsibilities to test, not necessarily four separate files. Start with a compact coherent Skill before splitting it.

## Experiment protocol

- Before the competition key arrives, use official manual CLI/MCP play to understand the interface and collect questions. Do not describe manual play as a benchmark of submitted Skills.
- Once the key is available, run the submitted agent with the official repository, JiuwenSwarm 0.2.4b3, single-agent mode and the designated model.
- Establish a balanced baseline, then compare one strategy change at a time: more recovery, more career growth, or more attention to warning feedback.
- Aim for at least three independent complete runs per tested version, subject to time and credit. This is an initial comparison, not a guarantee of statistical significance.
- Record the exact version, run identifier, months survived, quantitative score, ending contributions, performance history, failure reason and token usage.
- Compare mean results and variability, not only the highest score. Event order and conditions may differ, so one run cannot establish causality.
- Keep operational runtime logs out of Git. Summarize general failure patterns and design changes in English.
- Do not create event-to-answer tables, memorize event sequences, inspect package internals or present unpublished thresholds/formulas as known rules.

## Current evidence

Read [the complete exploratory findings](exploratory-findings.md) first. The main four-hour random baseline finished 73,858 sessions and 1,563,578 actions. No session reached month 48. Average survival was 5.91 months, and the average quantitative score was 11.78/100. Health exhaustion and trust crises accounted for 96.2% of recorded terminal outcomes.

A separate initial pilot of 30 random sessions preceded that batch. Its records are not included in the main totals. Neither experiment evaluated a reasoning model or submitted Skills. No competition API key was used. The aggregate evidence is tracked in [random-baseline-summary.json](experiments/random-baseline-summary.json).

The next hypothesis is that health preservation and warning-driven risk assessment improve survival compared with indiscriminate play. This is an untested strategy hypothesis; validate it using the required platform and model once the key is available. Further large random batches are lower priority than controlled comparisons of reasoned strategies.
